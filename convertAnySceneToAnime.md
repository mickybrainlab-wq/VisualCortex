VISUAL LANGUAGE

Every object must be defined first by clean, confident ink linework before any color is added.

Use strong silhouette readability so the character remains recognizable even as a solid black shape.

All boundaries between forms must remain visible.

Hair strands, clothing folds, facial features, fingers, armor edges, weapons, architecture, trees, rocks, clouds, and background objects must have crisp line definitions.

Never allow shading or lighting to erase or blur these boundaries.

The drawing should still look complete even if all colors are removed.

LINE SYSTEM

Use professional anime line hierarchy.

• Thick outer contour lines
• Medium structural lines
• Thin internal detail lines
• Extremely sharp facial features
• Clean eye outlines
• Hair divided into readable sections
• Clothing folds clearly separated
• Mechanical parts precisely outlined

Lines should taper naturally and vary in thickness.

No fuzzy edges.

No painterly brush blending.

No soft smudging.

────────────────────────

COLOR SYSTEM

Apply authentic anime cel shading.

Use flat base colors first.

Add only 2–4 controlled shadow layers.

Every shadow must stay inside the boundaries created by the line art.

Never paint over or hide outlines.

Shadows should describe form rather than texture.

Use large readable shadow shapes instead of noisy gradients.

Keep colors saturated but controlled.

────────────────────────

LIGHTING

Single dominant cinematic light source.

Highlights are independent from shadows.

Highlights sit ON TOP of the colors instead of replacing them.

Use clean white or slightly warm highlights.

Metal uses sharp reflective bands.

Skin uses soft controlled highlights.

Eyes contain layered reflections:

• catchlight
• iris glow
• pupil depth
• subtle moisture reflection

Hair highlights follow strand direction.

Never overexpose.
